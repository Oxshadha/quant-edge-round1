# Scale-aware tails beat flat Gaussian risk

Worldwide empirical work on wavelet–copula and related multi-scale dependence shows that **tail co-movement is horizon-specific**, not a single number you can read off one correlation matrix: for macro-linked and many equity settings, **average and lower-tail dependence often strengthen from daily noise bands toward monthly–quarterly scales**, yet **GFC-style shocks ratchet dependence up at almost every scale while COVID-style panic concentrates new tail linkage at the shortest bands (roughly 2–8 days)**. Ignoring that structure pushes **VaR and ES wrong**—typically too optimistic in calm periods or failing coverage when crises reweight risk to frequencies a single-scale Gaussian copula cannot see—while wavelet-denoised or scale-matched pipelines **improve VaR/ES backtests qualitatively** even when published papers rarely quote a universal percentage improvement ([Aloui et al., 2013](https://ideas.repec.org/p/dpc/wpaper/0413.html); [Aloui & Jammazi, 2015](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.physa.2015.05.036); [Alqaralleh & Canepa, 2021](https://doi.org/10.3390/jrfm14070329)). For a same-day **SAIFA Quant Edge Round 1** replication on **US sector ETFs**, the literature supports locking **MODWT (Daubechies db2, J=6) on GARCH-t standardized residuals**, **AIC choice among Clayton / Gaussian / Student-t** on **three MRA horizon bands**, **Monte Carlo 1-day VaR and ES at 95% and 99%**, and an **out-of-sample benchmark of a single-horizon Gaussian copula** on the same margins—because that contrast isolates exactly what academia claims industry still misses: **horizon-matched tail dependence versus one Gaussian surface**.

## Tail dependence climbs with horizon until crises rewrite the map

The cross-country wavelet–copula record does **not** support a law that lower-tail dependence λ_L always rises with scale, but the **modal pattern** in daily MODWT and Haar à trous studies is clear: **fine-scale (D1, roughly 2–4 days) links are weak or insignificant**, while **mid and coarse details (D2–D6, up to about 64–128 days) carry stronger and more stable tail dependence**, especially for oil–equity and oil–commodity pairs ([Cai et al., 2020](https://www.mdpi.com/1996-1073/13/2/294); [Jammazi & Reboredo, 2016](https://ideas.repec.org/a/eee/energy/v107y2016icp866-888.html); [Pal & Mitra, 2019](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S014098831930026X)). European equity work finds **long-run lower and upper tail paths generally higher and less volatile than short-run paths**, with long-run peaks around **0.6** for stressed sovereign–market pairs while short-run paths **jump in crises** ([Shahzad et al., 2016](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.physa.2016.03.048)). **Asymmetry is normal**: lower tails dominate equity and commodity stress narratives, but **upper tails matter for some pairs**, and **copula family choice (SJC, Joe–Clayton, BB7) exists precisely to separate τ_L from τ_U** ([Cai et al., 2020](https://www.mdpi.com/2071-1050/13/2/294); [Carvalho et al., 2021](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8150631/)). Dissent is real but narrow: **D1-only zeros** (oil–East Asia), **pair-specific insignificance under HTW versus significance under VMD**, and **China A-share structures where short-horizon tail dependence persists but fades at longer holding periods** show that decomposition and market architecture matter as much as “horizon” as an abstract label ([Cai et al., 2020](https://www.mdpi.com/1996-1073/13/2/294); [Shahzad et al., 2016](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.physa.2016.03.048); [Fang & Egan, 2021](https://ideas.repec.org/a/wly/ijfiec/v26y2021/i1p1469-1487.html)). For **portfolio market risk**, the actionable reading is not a single λ curve but **two monitoring layers**: **tactical (D1–D3, contagion and rebalance frequency)** versus **strategic (D5–S_J, structural correlation breakdown)** ([Berger, 2015](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.physa.2015.05.053); [Gallegati, 2012](https://ideas.repec.org/a/eee/csdana/v56y2012i11p3491-3497.html)).

## GFC ratchets all scales; COVID spikes the shortest frequencies

Crisis episodes are where multi-horizon tail models earn their keep. After **Lehman (15 September 2008)**, oil–stock and US equity studies document **higher correlation and tail dependence at all wavelet scales**, not a pure high-frequency contagion blip—pre-crisis **asymmetric lower tails mainly at long run** give way to **both tails elevated post-shock**, and diversification benefits **collapse especially at long horizons** ([Jammazi & Reboredo, 2016](https://ideas.repec.org/a/eee/energy/v107y2016icp866-888.html); [Berger, 2015](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.physa.2015.05.053)). **Greece–Europe** work aligns: **short-run lower tail paths surge in the GFC** while **long-run interdependence with Greece stays high** ([Shahzad et al., 2016](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.physa.2016.03.048)). **COVID presents a different scale profile**: in WC-GARCH / wavelet-scale Clayton work on major markets, **pre-pandemic tail correlation was often low up to about 16 days** but **material at 32–128 days** (for example US–Japan roughly **0.5 at D5 rising toward ~0.8 at D6**), whereas during COVID **tail dependence jumps at D1–D2 (2–8 days) for all markets** while **D5–D6 can stay flat or fall**—a **reweighting of systematic risk toward high frequencies** that a long-horizon-only copula **systematically underestimates** ([Alqaralleh & Canepa, 2021](https://doi.org/10.3390/jrfm14070329)). US **sector** evidence without wavelets still shows **pandemic tail dependence up, with λ_L rising more than λ_U** for broad market versus sectors ([Kim & Jung, 2023](https://ideas.repec.org/a/taf/apeclt/v30y2023i4p510-515.html)). A replication that spans **2020 onward out-of-sample** therefore tests both **academic mechanism** and **competition narrative**: the wavelet–copula model should **not** win only on pre-2019 calm data if COVID is the stress that exposes single-scale Gaussian tails.

## Single-scale Gaussian copula VaR leaves tail severity on the table

When dependence is forced into **one Gaussian copula on full-sample GARCH residuals**, **joint extreme down moves are understated** relative to Clayton, Student-t, or vine structures with tail parameters—simulation studies warn that **Gaussian copula VaR tends to underestimate**, **t-copula helps modestly**, and **rich vines can overfit out-of-sample** ([Communications for Statistical Applications and Methods, 2021](http://www.csam.or.kr/journal/view.html?doi=10.29220%2FCSAM.2021.28.1.059); [CEPR DP5652](http://cepr.org/index%2Ephp/publications/dp5652)). Wavelet hybrids add a **second error**: using **long-run tail estimates for a 1-day rebalance horizon** (or **daily noise for a strategic allocation**) misaligns **true scale-specific λ_L** with the **risk measure’s holding period**. **Oil–East Asia SJC work** quantifies the economic side: **portfolio variance and ES reduction from hedging** stays near **~0.28–0.30 on raw and D1 scales** but **falls toward ~0.06 variance ratio at D6**—heterogeneous horizons change **optimal hedge ratios**, not just VaR labels ([Cai et al., 2020](https://www.mdpi.com/1996-1073/13/2/294)). **Wavelet-denoised oil–FX models beat raw-series VaR and ES** ([Aloui & Jammazi, 2015](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.physa.2015.05.036)); **agricultural macro-factor work** reports **improved VaR accuracy versus traditional approaches** ([Aloui et al., 2013](https://ideas.repec.org/p/dpc/wpaper/0413.html)). What is **missing** in the literature—and what Round 1 should supply—is a **clean Kupiec / Christoffersen / McNeil–Frey table** for an **equal-weight US sector ETF basket**: **MODWT-scale copula versus Gaussian copula** with **identical GARCH-t margins** and **identical OOS rolling protocol** ([Kupiec, 1995](https://www.pm-research.com/content/iijderiv/3/2/73); [Christoffersen, 1998](https://www.newyorkfed.org/medialibrary/media/research/epr/98v04n3/9810lope.pdf); [McNeil & Frey, 2000](https://faculty.washington.edu/ezivot/econ589/EVT_Mcneil_Frey_2000.pdf)). Methodologically, **MODWT dominates decimated DWT** for daily finance because it is **shift-invariant and variance-preserving** ([Cai et al., 2020](https://www.mdpi.com/2071-1050/13/2/294)); **J = 6** maps details to **~2 through ~128 days** plus smooth **S_6** for longer trend ([Cai et al., 2020](https://www.mdpi.com/2071-1050/13/2/294); [Mensi et al., 2017](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.eneco.2017.06.007)). The **canonical econometric order** is **GARCH (often GJR) with skew-t or t margins → MODWT on standardized residuals → copula per scale (SJC / Joe–Clayton / Clayton / t) → simulation for VaR, ES, or hedge ratios** ([Cai et al., 2020](https://www.mdpi.com/2071-1050/13/2/294); [Shahzad et al., 2016](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.physa.2016.03.048)); a **WC-GARCH variant decomposes returns first**, which matters when citing COVID contagion papers ([Alqaralleh & Canepa, 2021](https://doi.org/10.3390/jrfm14070329)).

## Regulators bucket horizons; wavelets estimate them continuously

Industry and Basel practice **acknowledge horizon heterogeneity** but **do not estimate scale-varying copulas**. **FRTB IMA** replaces **10-day VaR** with **97.5% ES on a 10-day base**, then scales via **liquidity horizons {10, 20, 40, 60, 120 days}** by risk-factor class; **large-cap equity price factors sit at 10 days**, with **stressed 12-month joint calibration** and **50/50 blending** of unconstrained versus risk-class-constrained ES ([BIS MAR33](https://www.bis.org/committees/bcbs/basel-framework/standard/mar/33/inforce/2023-01-01/published/2020-03-27)). **ES was chosen for tail severity and subadditivity**, not because it is easier to estimate—**short samples make ES noisy**, and critics note **coherence does not imply stable forecasts** ([BIS Explanatory Note](https://www.dirittobancario.it/wp-content/uploads/sites/default/files/allegati/explanatory_note.pdf); [Danielsson et al., VoxEU](http://cepr.org/index%2Ephp/voxeu/columns/why-risk-hard-measure)). Day-to-day desks still lean on **historical simulation with joint resampling**, **variance–covariance**, and **Gaussian or Student-t copulas on one chosen horizon**, plus **stress overlays**—**wavelet–copula stacks remain research-grade** because of **validation burden, PLA governance, and tail data scarcity multiplied by each scale** ([BIS MAR33.9–33.10](https://www.bis.org/committees/bcbs/basel-framework/standard/mar/33/inforce/2023-01-01/published/2020-03-27); [EBA MR benchmarking, 2017](https://www.eba.europa.eu/sites/default/files/documents/10180/15947/5c829f1b-b23a-477e-ad48-972b50a7de10/EBA%20Report%20results%20from%20the%202016%20market%20risk%20benchmarking%20%20-%20March%202017.pdf)). The **gap is institutional, not statistical**: wavelet–copula answers **how λ_L differs between ~5-day and ~60-day economic fluctuations**; FRTB answers **capital accounting with five discrete liquidity buckets**. For competition framing, that makes the **Gaussian single-horizon benchmark** a fair stand-in for **legacy internal engines**, while the **wavelet–copula arm** tests whether **academic scale effects survive** on **liquid sector ETFs** with **standard Python tooling** (`arch`, `modwtpy`, `copulae`, `yfinance`) ([ARCH docs](https://arch.readthedocs.io/en/stable/univariate/univariate_volatility_forecasting.html); [modwtpy](https://github.com/pistonly/modwtpy); [copulae](https://pypi.org/project/copulae/)).

## Conclusion

The literature converges on a **mechanism**—**dependence and tail dependence are functions of investment horizon and crisis regime**—more than on a **single numeric VaR uplift**, because **pair type, decomposition (MODWT versus HTW versus VMD), and copula family** shift estimated λ enough that overclaiming a universal scale slope would be wrong. What **does** transfer to **US sector portfolio risk** is the **evaluation design**: **match the copula’s effective horizon to the risk measure (1-day VaR/ES tied to short MRA bands)**, **report tail parameters by band**, **stress the OOS window with COVID**, and **pit against a Gaussian copula that uses the same GARCH-t margins but no scale split**—the comparison regulators and risk managers implicitly care about when they **stress correlations** without estimating **λ(h)**. Round 1 should treat **multi-scale tail tables and backtest pass rates** as primary outputs; **regulatory ES capital** remains a **Phase 2 mapping** onto **FRTB liquidity buckets**, not something wavelet papers have yet closed.
